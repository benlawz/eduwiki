# Self-Determination Theory and Motivation in Education

## Overview

Self-Determination Theory (SDT) provides a comprehensive framework for understanding human motivation, particularly in educational contexts. The theory identifies three basic psychological needs that, when satisfied, lead to intrinsic motivation and optimal functioning: autonomy, competence, and relatedness.

## Key Findings

- **Autonomy support** from teachers significantly increases students' intrinsic motivation and engagement
- **Competence** is fostered through optimal challenge and constructive feedback
- **Relatedness** with teachers and peers creates a supportive learning environment
- **External rewards** can undermine intrinsic motivation when they're perceived as controlling
- **Internalization** of extrinsic motivation occurs when the three needs are supported

## Methodology

SDT has been tested through hundreds of studies across multiple domains, including education. Research methods include:
- Longitudinal studies tracking motivation changes over time
- Experimental studies manipulating autonomy support vs. control
- Cross-cultural studies validating the theory across different societies
- Meta-analyses synthesizing findings from multiple studies

## Implications for Learning

This research directly supports our focus on [[Agency]] and [[Motivation]]. When students feel:
- **Autonomous** (they have choice and voice in their learning)
- **Competent** (they can succeed with effort)
- **Related** (they belong and are supported)

They become intrinsically motivated learners who persist through challenges and engage deeply with content.

## Practical Applications

- **Give students choices** in what, how, and when they learn
- **Provide optimal challenge** - tasks that are difficult but achievable
- **Offer constructive feedback** that focuses on effort and strategy
- **Create supportive relationships** between teachers and students
- **Avoid controlling language** and external rewards for basic tasks
- **Help students internalize** the value of learning activities

## Critical Considerations

- SDT applies across cultures but may manifest differently
- The theory doesn't suggest eliminating all structure or guidance
- Individual differences exist in how strongly people respond to need satisfaction
- Context matters - some situations may require more external regulation
